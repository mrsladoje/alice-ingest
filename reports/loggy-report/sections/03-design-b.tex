\subsection{Watching the platform}\label{sec:design-watch}

Everything that watches the platform is pushed up, never polled down. Every collector ships a health sample every 30~seconds. The poller samples the cluster at the same cadence and reads the roster of collectors that must run: the fleet.

Three lanes read the numbers: threshold monitors catch the cliffs we know, detectors catch unfamiliar shapes, and trend monitors catch slow drift. The numbers are health samples, and counts, error rates and lags from the log records.

A monitor is a saved rule the cluster runs on a schedule, writing an alert when its condition holds. An alert carries one of two tiers. A page wakes a person. A warn waits for one. A storage disk above 92~\% raises a page, and above 85 up to 92~\% raises a warn. An entity is one machine, or one log family on one machine: the thing a rule is about.

A detector is a scheduled Random Cut Forest model \cite{ref:rcf}. It learns a number's usual shape and grades each window from 0 to 1 by how unusual it is. Seventeen run, one model per entity. A volume detector reads a silent host as zero records, so it can grade silence. One monitor reads the grades and raises one page for the fleet when any passes 0.7. One forecaster, on disk fill only, trains on a week of hourly points and predicts a day ahead.

Every lane compares a recent window with the stretch of time before it, so what counts as normal moves with the farm. A threshold monitor checks every minute or every ten minutes against a fixed limit. A fast detector reads one-minute windows and a slow one 30-minute windows, and each point joins the last eight windows. A trend rule compares three ten-minute slices with the previous seven days. Tables~\ref{tab:detectors} and~\ref{tab:trendrules} in Section~\ref{sec:metrics} list every detector and trend rule. A detector keeps no fixed number of days. It holds a rolling sample of recent points and forgets older ones gradually, so a new level of traffic becomes normal after a while. The length of that memory follows from the detection plugin's defaults, which we neither changed nor measured. The trend rules cover what a detector forgets: they compare against a fixed seven days, so a slow drift that a detector absorbs still fires.

Thirty monitors run: 13 every minute and four every ten minutes on thresholds and detector results. Nine are trend comparisons, two run hourly on templates, two watch the projector and Alertmanager themselves. The two template rules are the detection on the log text today (Section~\ref{sec:logtext}).

The projector reads every alert and detector result every 30~seconds (Figure~\ref{fig:alerts}). A signal is one stored row: which rule or detector fired, on which entity, when. An episode is one trouble on one thing, with a start and an end. The projector stores each episode as one incident document, which groups the signals of one alert name on one entity. An episode closes after consecutive healthy windows: one for a threshold monitor, three for a trend monitor or detector. A grade above 0.5 opens an episode.

\begin{figure}[tbp]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{fig06-alerts.pdf}\caption{Pushed samples feed three lanes, the projector makes episodes, Alertmanager decides when a person is told, and the receiver stores what was sent.}\label{fig:alerts}\end{figure}

Alertmanager owns notification semantics only: grouping timers, silences and routing \cite{ref:alertmanager}. It forgets an alert five minutes after the last post, so the projector re-posts every open episode every 30~seconds. It holds a page 30~seconds and a warn five minutes, then sends one batch to the receiver. Nothing pages a human today.

The two monitors that watch the projector and Alertmanager post straight to the receiver, because that part can be dead. That is the break-glass path. The stale-projector monitor fires after ten minutes without a projector heartbeat. The other fires after the projector reports Alertmanager down for five minutes.

\subsection{Walkthrough: a dead collector becomes a notification}\label{sec:design-walk-dead}

A dead collector is silent, so the platform reasons from absence (Figure~\ref{fig:alerts}).

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item While it runs, each collector writes one health sample to \code{cockpit-metrics} every 30~seconds. The collector dies, and its samples stop.
\item Every 30~seconds the poller reads the roster and the samples of the last 90~seconds. It writes one fleet row per rostered collector to \code{cockpit-metrics}. The dead collector's row marks its heartbeat missing.
\item The collector-down monitor reads those rows every minute, one bucket per collector. It writes a page-tier alert that names the collector into the alerting plugin's alert index.
\item Within 30~seconds the projector reads the alert. It writes a signal row to \code{alice-signals} and opens an episode in \code{alice-incidents}.
\item The projector posts the open episode to Alertmanager, and posts it again every 30~seconds. Alertmanager forgets an alert five minutes after the last post, so the re-posts keep it alive.
\item Alertmanager groups the alert on alert name and collector, waits 30~seconds, and posts one batch to the receiver.
\item The receiver writes one document to \code{alice-notifications}. That document records that a person should be told.
\end{enumerate}

The cadences bound death-to-document at about 90 to 240~seconds. The first missing flag arrives 60 to 120~seconds after the death. No run has timed it (Section~\ref{sec:limits-unmeasured}).

\subsection{Walkthrough: an EPN goes quiet, or loud}\label{sec:design-walk-trend}

The trend rules never scan raw lines when they check. They compare small rows with seven days of the same rows (Figure~\ref{fig:alerts}).

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Every ten minutes the rollup service waits 120~seconds for late records. Then it reads the last three finished ten-minute slices from the log indices.
\item It writes one row per family and entity per slice to \code{trend-rollup}. An entity is a host, or a collector for the lags. Each row holds record, error and fatal counts and the 95th percentile of two lags. The lags are the line's age when the collector read it, and its trip from there into the store.
\item For an entity that logged in the last 24~hours but not in this slice, the rollup writes a zero-count row. An entity silent for longer gets none. A commit row closes the slice when every row has landed.
\item Every ten minutes the nine trend monitors read those rows. Each compares the three latest slices, ending ten minutes before the check, with the same entity over the previous seven days. With no seven-day history, a monitor falls back to 24~hours.
\item A volume rule compares the host's share of its fleet, so a fleet-wide ramp cancels. It fires on twice the baseline share or half of it, in all three slices. A rise needs 50~records in every slice, and every rule needs six baseline slices.
\item The two shipping-lag rules compare the entity's own lag with twice its seven-day baseline, floored at 250~ms. Each writes a warn-tier alert when it fires.
\item A separate monitor writes a page-tier alert when a whole family stops. It fires when a family averaging at least 50~records a slice over 24~hours writes nothing for 40~minutes.
\item The projector turns each alert into a signal and an episode, as in Section~\ref{sec:design-walk-dead}. Alertmanager holds warn-tier alerts five minutes and batches them by rule name. It also splits the shipping-lag batches by collector.
\end{enumerate}

Which messages changed is the template lane's question (Section~\ref{sec:design-templates}).

\subsection{The surfaces}\label{sec:design-surfaces}

A person reaches all four surfaces through one web address and one account. Dashboards, the store's own web interface, holds the maintainer cockpit and Discover, its search screen for the archive \cite{ref:opensearch}. The shifter view is separate. Its live page keeps what collectors post: every InfoLogger record, the daemon log and every line above info severity. It never queries the cluster, so it holds when the cluster is down. Its Templates page shows the catalogue, counts and open episodes.

Two clocks run. Health samples and the log-counting detectors use the collector's accept time. Discover uses the time the line was written. The operator view close to InfoLogger is not built.
