The ALICE Event Processing Nodes write logs that mostly never reach the tool people read with. \loggy{} keeps them on the worker that wrote them and searches every worker as one. One collector per worker reads six log sources and stamps each line with a template. Info, debug and trace stay in one local copy. Every other record goes to three storage machines. Every worker pushes its health up. Nothing polls it. Threshold rules, Random Cut Forest detectors and trend rules turn numbers into episodes. It runs on five staging machines. A farm deployment of the cluster, the collectors and Dashboards passed every check. In the busiest hour of six months of archive the median worker carried 23~records a second, the busiest worker-second 78. The collector costs about a quarter of one core at 20,000 records a second. The whole stack on one worker sustains about 42,000 a second. A flush interval of 1~second instead of 5 cut the collector's cost 24.5~\% and its peak memory 28~\%. The templating pipeline is 31 to 57~\% cheaper per family and byte-identical on 8,961,245 lines. Dense retrieval with reranking reaches 0.685 nDCG@10 against 0.634 for the scan alone. The main limit: every cost figure comes from a laptop. Only the shape of a curve and the ranking of settings transfer to the farm.
